

## Discussion
### Model performance limitations
The model demonstrates moderate discrimination (ROC AUC slightly above chance), but the ability to correctly identify mortality cases remains limited:
- **Low recall for deaths** indicates the model misses many mortality events.
- **Low precision** indicates many predicted deaths are false alarms.

This pattern is expected for baseline models on imbalanced clinical data.

### Why accuracy can look acceptable
Accuracy is influenced by the majority class (alive). With class imbalance, a model may predict the majority class more often and still achieve a relatively high accuracy, while performing poorly on the minority class.

---

## How to interpret precision vs cost in practice
In real hospital operations, the decision threshold is not inherently 0.5; it depends on the clinical and operational costs of errors.

### Precision improvement (reduce false alarms)
To improve **precision**:
- increase the decision threshold so fewer patients are predicted as high-risk,
- potentially use precision-oriented threshold selection and/or class-imbalance handling.

Effect: fewer false alarms, but possibly lower recall.

---

### Cost-balanced approach (choose threshold by error cost)
A cost-balanced approach selects the probability threshold based on the relative severity of:
- **False Negatives** (missed deaths) — potentially high clinical risk
- **False Positives** (unnecessary escalation) — operational cost, typically lower medical risk than missing a death

A practical strategy is to sweep thresholds and select one that optimizes a defined cost function or an operational trade-off.

---

### Balanced F1 (trade-off between precision & recall)
To balance precision and recall directly, this project tunes the operational threshold by:
1. computing precision-recall across candidate thresholds,
2. selecting the threshold that **maximizes F1-score** for mortality (Class 1).

This provides a defensible threshold choice when precision and recall are both important.
